## What each paper studied (from secondary sources)

The series is one continuous experimental program on pictorial balance with balance as the dependent variable and color/form/size as manipulated factors:
- **Part 1 (1983):** color × size in balance judgments.
- **Part 2 (1984):** color × form in balance judgments.
- **Part 3 (1985):** color × size in balance, continued ("2").

Its reference list cites Part 1 (1983, 34(3), 1–6) and Part 2 (1984, 35(3), 76–84 [sic]) directly — confirming the series numbering and the color/size → color/form ordering.

### Method (own-words summary)
B5 sheet used landscape; a square is printed on the left half (4 variants A–D: left-edge distance and size — 5 cm vs 6 cm — varied; printed in black, gray, or white = 3 lightness levels; 12 sheet types). The subject places a 3 cm colored square (blue, green, yellow, or red — one color per experiment session) on the right half of the sheet so as to achieve "good harmony" with the printed square, then fixes it with tape. The measured variable is *d*: the distance in mm from the placed square's right edge to the frame's right edge. Subjects: Doshisha Women's College psychology students (n = 106–154 per experiment) and Osaka University of Arts "造形心理学" students (n = 62–93), 4 weekly sessions, ~48 judgments per subject.

### Findings — 8 conclusions (own-words summary)
1. **Larger printed figure → placed figure moves farther right** ("kicked farther"): A>B and C>D held in every table — size of the fixed figure uniformly displaces the balance point outward.
2. **Kick strength decreases black → gray → white** (clearest for blue; murkier for green/yellow/red — "colors have their own character").
3. **The placed color square shows color-specific "resistance"** (レジスタンス): blue is kicked farthest, then green, then yellow; **red resists most** (placed innermost). I.e., under this paradigm red behaves as visually heaviest, blue as lightest of the four.
4. Black's clarity drives the placement effect; gray/white are too ambiguous to produce clear color differences.
5. Groups need n ≥ 100 for stable results.
6. Temperament differences between subject groups matter.
7. Despite individual differences, common (normative) tendencies exist — "preferences have a standard value."
8. Art-aspiring students are more individualistic: larger variance around the mean.

### Why it matters for the formula question
This is an **equilibrium-position paradigm**: the placement point is where the printed figure's "kick" (a repulsive visual force ∝ its visual weight) balances the color square's "resistance" (∝ its own visual weight) — a literal force-balance model of pictorial balance producing quantitative *d* values as functions of **size** (5 vs 6 cm), **lightness** (black/gray/white), and **hue** (blue/green/yellow/red). Parts 1–3 are the 1983–85 runs of this same program; the 1993 paper is its matured continuation.
