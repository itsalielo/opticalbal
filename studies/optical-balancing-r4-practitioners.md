## 1. New verified maker-attributed optical-correction cases in identity/logo work

### 1a. Twitter bird, 2012 — Martin Grasser (with Todd Waterbury & Angy Che)
**What was adjusted:** The bird was constructed from three sets of overlapping circles (the "underlying neutrality and simplicity" geometry), then hand-perfected detail by detail until it *felt* balanced — geometry was the scaffold, the eye made the final calls. It was also tuned to read "as a bird at the smallest of sizes, almost like a lowercase 'e.'"
**Who said it:** Martin Grasser, one of the three designers, on the record twice: (i) in his own LinkedIn post "The Bird" (2023), reproduced verbatim by AdNews — "From that point on we really spent our time perfecting every little detail... so that it felt balanced, and visible as a bird at the smallest of sizes"; (ii) Digiday interview (2023) — "Once you agree on the construction method, that is really what driving the shape... What gives the bird whatever charm it has is that it's constructed by that single shape."
**Sources:** https://www.adnews.com.au/news/listenin-how-the-now-dead-twitter-bird-logo-came-to-life · https://digiday.com/media/twitters-original-logo-designer-explains-why-the-bluebird-worked-so-well-and-how-x-changes-everything/
**Note:** The strongest single case of the round for the mission: it documents the exact bounded task — geometric construction followed by deliberate by-eye optical rebalancing. The circles were the starting system, not the finish.

### 1b. Mailchimp rebrand, 2018 — Collins (agency) + Mailchimp in-house design team
**What was adjusted:** The script wordmark was replaced with a custom bold sans wordmark deliberately engineered to sit "in harmony" with the Freddie icon — a direct symbol–wordmark weight-matching task. Freddie himself was redrawn as a simplified single-colour silhouette so the two could appear together.
**Who said it:** Mailchimp (the client's design leadership), quoted in Creative Bloq: "Through a process of iteration and refinement, we've developed a wordmark that lives in harmony with the Freddie icon to build equity for both." Also: "Our beloved logo script and Freddie icon had hierarchy issues and never appeared together."
**Source:** https://www.creativebloq.com/news/mailchimp-rebrand-does-away-with-script-wordmark
**Note:** Maker-attributed, explicit statement of the symbol–wordmark weight-matching task as a design *problem* (hierarchy issues between symbol and wordmark) solved by iteration.

### 1c. Mailchimp script facelift, 2013 — Jessica Hische
**What was adjusted:** A restrained optical facelift of the original script: much of the lettering in "Chimp" was opened up (reducing the scrawling density, improving small-size legibility), and the baseline was straightened to make the signature "more regular and precise" while keeping the friendly feel.
**Who said it:** Jessica Hische, in her own process write-up on her website, quoted by Fast Company: "They just wanted a facelift–one of those classy facelifts that make your friends ask you if you've been sleeping better lately or lost some weight because you look like a more vivacious version of yourself and not like a different person."
**Source:** https://www.fastcompany.com/1672938/the-anatomy-of-a-successful-logo-redesign
**Note:** Well-known case; included because it is not on the Round 2 exclusion list. Optical correction as invisible restraint — the corrections are meant to be felt, not seen.

### 1d. Warner Bros. shield, 2019 — Emily Oberman, Pentagram
**What was adjusted:** The "WB" monogram was redrawn "to give the letterforms a better sense of balance," and the shield itself was thinned out and set to classical (golden-ratio) proportions. Minor tweaks rather than reinvention — "small tweaks to a well-established visual identity" as "recalibration."
**Who said it:** Emily Oberman, Pentagram partner leading the project, in a Fast Company interview.
**Source:** https://www.fastcompany.com/90429745/warner-brotherss-new-logo-and-brand-are-a-glimpse-at-the-future-of-entertainment?ref=freshfordesigners.com
**Note:** Explicit maker-attributed "redrawn for balance" statement on a monogram — the same class of task as symbol–wordmark weight matching (internal weight rebalancing of a letterform mark).

### 1e. Instagram icon, 2016 — Ian Spalter, Instagram head of design (weight statement, not a correction)
**What was adjusted:** N/A as a correction — but Spalter is on the record judging a redesign *by visual weight*: the early flattening explorations of the camera icon "lacked the visual weight of the original."
**Who said it:** Ian Spalter, in a Dezeen interview about the 2016 redesign process.
**Source:** https://www.dezeen.com/2016/05/11/instagram-new-logo-graphic-design-camera-rainbow/amp/
**Note:** Tier 2. Not an optical-correction case per se; filed here as maker-attributed evidence that senior identity practitioners evaluate lockup/icon redesigns in explicit "visual weight" terms.

## 2. The symbol–wordmark weight-matching task: practitioner writing

Round 2 identified the recurring bounded task: matching a symbol's visual weight to its wordmark entirely by eye. Round 4 found:

- **Mailchimp 2018 (above):** the clearest maker-attributed formulation — a wordmark "developed... to live in harmony with the Freddie icon," solving "hierarchy issues" between the two. No published measurements; the method was "iteration and refinement."
- **Practitioner rules of thumb (practitioner-authored essay, Tier 2):** Brand designer Khairin Kamarizal's own lockup-design notes: "size the symbol so that its optical height is equal to the cap height of the wordmark. Adjust from there based on the visual weight of each element. A very bold, heavy symbol may need to be slightly smaller than cap height to avoid overwhelming the wordmark. A fine, delicate symbol may need to be slightly larger to hold its presence." Spacing derived from a cap-height unit.
  Source: https://github.com/khairinkamarizal/portfolio/blob/HEAD/content/writing/brand-identity-lockup-design-476.md
- **Practitioner rule of thumb (practitioner-authored, Tier 2):** Graham Smith (imjustcreative) on logo construction: after the idea is drawn, "things like the thickness of the logo mark matching the thickness of the letter M" — i.e., matching symbol stroke weight to wordmark stem weight by eye (Medicine.com case).
  Source: https://imjustcreative.com/logo-design-grids/2021/08/20
- **Instagram 2016 (above):** "visual weight of the original" as the benchmark for a redrawn icon.
- **Related, already found (Round 2, not re-reported):** Google "G" 2015, NBC peacock 2022.

**Finding:** Practitioners describe weight matching as an eye-driven calibration task with measurement *scaffolds* (cap height, stem thickness, spacing units) but no published formula — consistent with the mission's hypothesis that the formal decomposition (weight = f(saturation, hue, lightness, area, position, contrast)) has never been made explicit by makers.

## 3. Type-design optical compensation beyond overshoot: published numbers & rules on the record

### 3a. Peter Karow — overshoot numbers (the baseline)
**What:** Karow's manuals specify overshoot magnitudes: around **3% of cap height for an O**, about **5% for an A**.
**Who said it:** Published in Karow's own manuals (*Digital Formats for Typefaces*); figures cited in a secondary overview.
**Source:** https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/the-hidden-details-in-every-font-197535f19aad
**Note:** This is the overshoot baseline itself, not "beyond" it — included as the only widely published quantitative optical-correction values in type design.

### 3b. Erik Spiekermann — diagonal strokes must be drawn thinner
**What:** When completing Bauhaus geometric alphabets, "we had to deal with optical issues like the thickening of strokes where two meet, **diagonals that appear thicker than vertical strokes (they have to be thinner to look the same)**."
**Who said it:** Erik Spiekermann, direct interview (Creative Boom).
**Source:** https://www.creativeboom.com/work/erik-spiekermann/
**Note:** A qualitative-but-directional published rule for stroke-weight compensation — the kind of directional factor the six-factor fit needs.

### 3c. Erik Spiekermann — FF Meta, optical sizing for small sizes on bad paper
**What:** The Deutsche Bundespost brief (small sizes, bad paper) could not be met by any existing face: "By that time we knew what we needed: **the weight, the width, the optical issues**…"
**Who said it:** Erik Spiekermann, MyFonts Creative Characters interview.
**Source:** https://www.myfonts.com/de/pages/newsletters-cc-201412
**Note:** Maker-attributed statement that optical compensation was a named design variable at the brief stage.

### 3d. Tobias Frere-Jones — the eye-over-ruler principle (own blog, via Fast Company)
**What:** Overshoot rationale: "If the 'correct' height appears inadequate, 'too much' will look right. So the O is made taller and deeper than the H, even if the most stringent mathematical reasoning would declare it incorrect. **But we read with our eyes, not with rulers, so the eye should win every time.**"
**Who said it:** Tobias Frere-Jones, on his own blog, quoted by Fast Company.
**Source:** https://www.fastcompany.com/3042391/why-all-typefaces-are-optical-illusions

### 3e. Tobias Frere-Jones — small-size optical rules (Retina)
**What:** For small sizes: "**Let the letters breathe**... Having more space between letters will allow their shapes to be rendered and understood more clearly. There are other points like avoiding really thin features and fussy details."
**Who said it:** Tobias Frere-Jones, direct interview with Fast Company (on the Apple Watch typeface question, referencing his *Retina* face for the Wall Street Journal).
**Source:** https://www.fastcompany.com/90181754/what-the-font-analyzing-the-new-apple-watch-typeface-2/
**Note:** Maker-attributed optical-size compensation rules: spacing increase, feature simplification.

### 3f. Matthew Carter — Bell Centennial, size-specific compensation (his own foundry's page)
**What:** For AT&T phone books (6pt, high-speed newsprint): Carter **increased the x-height** of lowercase characters, **slightly condensed the character width**, **carved out many more open counters and bowls**, and drew **deep ink traps** to anticipate ink spread — "designed to fill in as the ink spread onto newsprint fiber, leaving the characters' counterforms open and legible at small point sizes."
**Who said it:** Type Network's Matthew Carter Collection page (Carter's own foundry).
**Sources:** https://typenetwork.com/articles/matthew-carter-collection · https://www.myfonts.com/pages/newsletters-cc-201310 (Creative Characters interview)
**Note:** The canonical maker-attributed case of optical compensation *beyond* overshoot: size- and medium-specific reshaping, documented in Carter's own foundry materials.

### 3g. Matthew Carter — optical sizes as product (Sitka, Role)
**What:** Microsoft's **Sitka** (2018): "a ground-breaking exercise in designing a typeface for reading, based on repeated readability tests and design iterations, **with six optical sizes**." Morisawa's **Role** (2019): serif, sans, slab and round subfamilies in nine weights and **three optical sizes**.
**Who said it:** Type Network's Matthew Carter Collection page.
**Source:** https://typenetwork.com/articles/matthew-carter-collection
**Note:** Maker-attributed evidence that optical-size compensation is productized as discrete, tested size masters.

### 3h. Fontwerk "Case" (Spiekermann, Meiners & du Carrois, 2020) — three optical sizes
**What:** Neo-grotesque in three optical sizes; the Text and Micro families feature a **higher x-height** and **more open shapes**; Micro adds "enhanced contrast at the joints of stems and bows."
**Who said it:** Publisher description (Fontwerk), reported by PRINT Magazine — maker-adjacent, Tier 2 (not a direct designer quote).
**Source:** https://www.printmag.com/type-tuesday/type-tuesday-the-curious-and-captivating-case-of-erik-spiekermann/
**Note:** Published optical-size deltas described qualitatively; exact numbers not located this round.

## 6. Summary for the mission

- **4 new verified maker-attributed optical-correction cases in identity work** (Twitter bird 2012, Mailchimp 2018, Mailchimp/Hische 2013, Warner Bros. 2019), plus 1 maker-attributed visual-weight statement (Instagram 2016).
- **Symbol–wordmark weight matching** is confirmed as the recurring bounded practitioner task; makers describe it in *weight/harmony* language and solve it by iteration, with cap-height/stem-thickness scaffolds — but no published formula, exactly the gap the mission targets.
- **Type design beyond overshoot:** the only widely published numbers remain Karow's overshoot figures (~3% O, ~5% A); everything else is directional rules (diagonals thinner, more air at small sizes, higher x-height, open apertures, ink traps) or productized optical-size masters (Sitka ×6, Role ×3, Case ×3). No maker has published per-factor weight coefficients — consistent with the computational track's finding that the exact formula is underdetermined without human-anchored weights.
