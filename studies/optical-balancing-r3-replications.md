# Optical Balancing — Round 3: Replication Scan

**Date:** 2026-10-09 · **Question:** have the key quantitative visual-weight models (DCM, APB, VME, McManus CoM, Birkhoff) been directly replicated, extended, or refuted since Rounds 1–2? Does any fMRI work on compositional balance now exist?

**Method.** Citation chaining via Semantic Scholar (citing-paper lists pulled 2026-10-09: Hübner & Fillinger 2016 → 43 citing; Wilson & Chatterjee 2005 → 107 citing; McManus/Stöver/Kim 2011 → 94 citing; Zhang & Xue 2025 → 1 citing), plus targeted web searches for fMRI and Birkhoff replication. Rule applied throughout: a citation is not a replication — only genuine empirical re-tests count. Every paper below was verified by exact-title search with DOI. Quotes kept minimal; summaries are in my own words.

## 1. DCM / center-of-mass balance measures — Hübner & Fillinger (2016)

*Comparison of Objective Measures for Predicting Perceptual Balance and Visual Aesthetic Preference*, Frontiers in Psychology 7:335. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00335](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00335)

43 citing papers. No independent group has directly re-run DCM's core experiments. What exists instead is a dense program of **extensions, almost all from the originating lab**, plus one strong independent empirical extension from HCI:

**Extensions by the originators**
- **Fillinger & Hübner (2020)**, "On the relation between perceived stability and aesthetic appreciation," *Acta Psychologica*. DOI: [10.1016/j.actpsy.2020.103082](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2020.103082). Disentangles gravitational stability from mechanical balance as predictors of liking.
- **Hübner & Fillinger (2019)**, "Perceptual Balance, Stability, and Aesthetic Appreciation: Their Relations Depend on the Picture Type," *i-Perception* 10(3). DOI: [10.1177/2041669519856040](https://doi.org/10.1177/2041669519856040). Key qualification: "balance" is read as mechanical equilibrium for single-element pictures but as gravitational stability for multi-element/dynamic ones; only the multi-element stimuli showed a balance→liking link.
- **Hübner & Thömmes (2019)**, "Symmetry and Balance as Factors of Aesthetic Appreciation: Ethel Puffer's (1903) 'Studies in Symmetry' Revised," *Symmetry* 11(12):1468. DOI: [10.3390/sym11121468](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym11121468). A deliberate replication of Puffer's 1903 production-method experiments with modern methods — and it found little to no evidence that balance drives preference. This is a null result published by the DCM authors themselves; it constrains the theory (DCM predicts perceived balance well, but perceived balance weakly predicts liking).
- **Hübner (2025)**, "Preference for symmetry, balance, or proximity in picture aesthetics depends on the method of evaluation," *i-Perception*. DOI: [10.1177/20416695251381548](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695251381548). Task-frame finding: rating tasks elicit symmetry/balance preference, production tasks elicit proximity — the compositional rule people follow depends on how you ask.
- **Thömmes & Hübner (2018)**, "Instagram Likes for Architectural Photos Can Be Predicted by Quantitative Balance Measures and Curvature," *Frontiers in Psychology*. DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01050](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01050). Real-world application: DCM-family measures predict ~700 Instagram likes.
- **Mutter & Hübner (2024)**, expertise effects on composition creation/evaluation, *Scientific Reports*. DOI: [10.1038/s41598-024-64494-7.

**Independent empirical extension (not the originating lab)**
- **Chen & Lu (2023)**, "Balanced Aesthetics: How Shape, Contrast, and Visual Force Affect Interface Layout," *International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction*. DOI: [10.1080/10447318.2023.2289294](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2023.2289294). Three experiments decomposing visual weight: vertical/horizontal ellipses shift weight while polygons and rounded rectangles don't; higher contrast raises weight (especially on white); visual force directed toward the interface center lowers weight, outward raises it. This is the closest thing to an independent empirical test of DCM-adjacent visual-weight factors — and it supports a factor-decomposition view.

**Dissemination / tooling (not empirical re-tests)**
- **Redies & Bartho (2025)**, "A toolbox for calculating quantitative image properties in aesthetics research," *Behavior Research Methods*. DOI: [10.3758/s13428-025-02632-3](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02632-3). The Aesthetics Toolbox standardizes balance measures for the field.
- **Van Geert & Bossens (2022)**, OCTA order/complexity toolbox, *Behavior Research Methods*. DOI: [10.3758/s13428-022-01900-w](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-022-01900-w).
- **Braun & Hofmann (2026)**, "Being K. Malevich: A hands-on approach to compositional preference," *i-Perception*. DOI: [10.1177/20416695261421103](https://doi.org/10.1177/20416695261421103). Production-method study of balance/stability/dynamics using Suprematist elements — conceptually in the DCM lineage, not a re-test.

**VERDICT: EXTENDED** — substantially extended by the originating lab (with honest null/qualification results) and independently in HCI by Chen & Lu (2023); but no independent group has directly replicated DCM's core experiments, and no refutation of the measure itself exists.

## 2. APB index — Wilson & Chatterjee (2005)

*The Assessment of Preference for Balance: Introducing a New Test*, Empirical Studies of the Arts 23(1). DOI: [10.2190/B1LR-MVF3-F36X-XR64](https://doi.org/10.2190/B1LR-MVF3-F36X-XR64)

107 citing papers, mostly background citations. One genuine empirical re-test exists, and it failed:

- **Gershoni & Hochstein (2011)**, "Measuring pictorial balance perception at first glance using Japanese calligraphy," *i-Perception*. DOI: [10.1068/i0472aap](https://doi.org/10.1068/i0472aap). The APB index had been validated on geometric shapes with unlimited viewing. Gershoni & Hochstein tested it against first-fixation balance perception of Japanese calligraphy, comparing APB scores with balance-rating and comparison tasks. Their finding: the behavioral tasks correlated highly with each other but showed low correlation with the APB index. This is the single direct empirical re-test of APB on record, and it does not support the index's generality — a boundary-condition failure on non-geometric stimuli under brief exposure.

**Related but not re-tests**
- **Chatterjee (2010)**, "The Assessment of Art Attributes," *Empirical Studies of the Arts*. DOI: [10.2190/EM.28.2.f](https://doi.org/10.2190/EM.28.2.f). Folds APB into a broader art-attribute test battery (application, not replication).
- **Menzel et al. (2018)**, "Visual mismatch negativity indicates automatic, task-independent detection of artistic image composition in abstract artworks," *Biological Psychology*. DOI: [10.1016/j.biopsycho.2018.05.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2018.05.005). EEG/ERP study citing Wilson & Chatterjee as background; tests composition detection, not the APB index.
- **Straffon et al. (2025)**, "Traces of Intentionality: Balance, Complexity, and Organization in Artworks by Humans and Apes," *Topics in Cognitive Science*. DOI: [10.1111/tops.70022](https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.70022). Uses subjective balance ratings (human vs. chimpanzee art), not the APB formula.
- **Myszkowski et al. (2017)** VAST-R and **Corradi et al. (2019)** aesthetic-sensitivity work: adjacent constructs, separate instruments.

**VERDICT: REFUTED (as a general index)** — the only genuine empirical re-test (Gershoni & Hochstein 2011) failed: APB scores did not track first-glance balance ratings of calligraphy. Caveat: it is a single study on a stimulus class far from APB's original geometric-shape validation, so this reads as a boundary failure rather than a wholesale demolition — but with zero successful independent replications on record, the index cannot be called replicated.

## 3. Visual Moment Equilibrium — Zhang & Xue (2025)

*Visual Moment Equilibrium: A Computational Cognitive Model for Assessing Visual Balance in Interface Layout Aesthetics*, Symmetry 18(1):41. DOI: [10.3390/sym18010041](https://doi.org/10.3390/sym18010041)

Exactly 1 citing paper:

- **Yuan, Wang & Zhong (2026)**, "Research on Optimal Design of Visual Elements of CNC Press Display Interface Based on Visual Cognition," *Applied System Innovation* 9(6):121. DOI: [10.3390/asi9060121](https://doi.org/10.3390/asi9060121). Cites the VME model in its literature review as an objective computational method for interface-layout balance — a passing background citation. It does not implement, test, or evaluate the VME model.

**VERDICT: UNREPLICATED** — one token citation, zero empirical re-tests, zero extensions, zero refutations. The model is too new and uncited to have been tested.

## 4. McManus / Stöver / Kim (2011) — Arnheim's CoM balance

*Arnheim's Gestalt theory of visual balance: Examining the compositional structure of art photographs and abstract images*, i-Perception. DOI: [10.1068/i0445aap](https://doi.org/10.1068/i0445aap)

94 citing papers. Core claim: centers of mass of art photographs (and expert/non-expert croppings) sit closer to a compositional axis than controls. The descriptive statistic has **never been directly re-tested by an independent group**; the aesthetic-consequence claim has been twice weakened by direct experiments:

**Direct empirical engagements (genuine re-tests of the equilibrium→aesthetics link)**
- **Samuel & Kerzel (2013)**, "Judging whether it is aesthetic: Does equilibrium compensate for the lack of symmetry?" *i-Perception*. DOI: [10.1068/i0557](https://doi.org/10.1068/i0557). Two experiments on rectangle compositions with the center of mass centered vs. off-center: CoM position influenced aesthetics ratings only slightly (it moved weight ratings more strongly), while the overall shape of the configuration dominated. A genuine empirical re-test with a largely null result for the aesthetic payoff of equilibrium.
- **Leyssen, Linsen, Sammartino & Palmer (2012)**, "Aesthetic preference for spatial composition in multiobject pictures," *i-Perception*. DOI: [10.1068/i0458aap](https://doi.org/10.1068/i0458aap). Five experiments on horizontal positioning in multi-object pictures: relatedness, separation, and inward biases dominated; only a weak balance effect survived in multiple regression. Independent, same-paradigm-family test: balance is a minor factor.

**Theoretical alternative (not peer-reviewed)**
- **Corwin (2020–, bioRxiv 10.1101/2020.05.26.104687, revised through v22 in 2023)**, "Pictorial balance is a bottom-up aesthetic property mediated by eye movements." Proposes a competing quadrant-luminance balance model (lower half lighter by ~1.07×) grounded in eye-movement ease rather than mechanics, with its own small experiments. Cites McManus 2011 but never replicates its analyses. Preprint only — no peer review, no independent uptake.

**Computational / applied extensions**
- **Jahanian (2015)**, "Learning visual balance from large-scale datasets of aesthetically highly rated images," *SPIE/IS&T Electronic Imaging*. DOI: [10.1117/12.2084548](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2084548), plus the 2016 Springer thesis *Quantifying aesthetics of visual design applied to automatic design*. Learns balance predictors from data rather than deriving them — a machine-learning extension of the balance program.
- **Park (2019)**, "Secrets of Balanced Composition as Seen through a Painter's Window," DOI: [10.1145/3306211.3320136](https://doi.org/10.1145/3306211.3320136). Subset-barycenter visualization tool for art-historical analysis — direct methodological descendant of CoM thinking.
- **Hoh et al. (2023)**, "Salient-Centeredness and Saliency Size in Computational Aesthetics," DOI: [10.1145/3588317](https://doi.org/10.1145/3588317). Finds a clear preference for centered dominant salient regions and that the rule of thirds is not generally valid — applied compositional-balance empirics.
- **Amirshahi et al. (2014)**, "Evaluating the Rule of Thirds in Photographs and Paintings," DOI: [10.1163/22134913-00002024](https://doi.org/10.1163/22134913-00002024). Behavioral ROT scores vs. computed saliency-map ROT: only moderate agreement — weakens the computational-balance shortcut.
- **Miton et al. (2020)**, "A Forward Bias in Human Profile-Oriented Portraits," *Cognitive Science*. DOI: [10.1111/cogs.12866](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12866). Corpus analysis of 1,831 paintings: more free space in front of sitters, both produced and preferred — a large-scale compositional-statist
